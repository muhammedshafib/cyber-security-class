# Understand Cyber Security Fundamentals

Cybersecurity is the foundation of your cybersecurity learning journey. Before studying ethical hacking, networking security, cloud security, or penetration testing, you should understand these five important concepts.

Let's learn each topic from beginner to advanced level, with simple explanations, real-world examples, and exam-oriented questions.

## a. What is Cyber Security?

Definition:

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, servers, applications, and data from cyberattacks, unauthorized access, damage, and theft.

In simple words, cybersecurity means protecting digital systems and information from hackers and other cyber threats.

Example:

Imagine you have a bank account with an online banking application.

- You use a password to log in.
- You receive an OTP for verification.
- The bank encrypts your financial information.
- Security systems monitor suspicious login activities.

All these security measures are part of cybersecurity.

Main goals of Cybersecurity: CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is one of the most important concepts in cybersecurity.

Confidentiality

Only authorized people can access information.

Integrity

Information must remain accurate and unchanged.

Availability

Information and systems must be accessible when needed.

Real-world example:

Consider an online banking system.

- Confidentiality: Only authorized users can view account details.
- Integrity: Nobody should modify your bank balance without authorization.
- Availability: You should be able to access banking services whenever required.

Why is the CIA Triad important?

It provides the three fundamental security objectives used when designing and evaluating security systems.

## c. Information Security vs Cyber Security

These two terms are closely related, but they are not exactly the same.

1\. Information Security (InfoSec)

Information Security is the practice of protecting information from unauthorized access, modification, disclosure, destruction, or loss.

It protects information in both digital and physical formats.

Example:

- Protecting digital files.
- Securing paper documents.
- Controlling access to confidential records.
- Protecting employee information.

2\. Cyber Security

Cybersecurity focuses on protecting digital systems, networks, devices, and information from cyber threats and attacks.

Example:

- Protecting computers from malware.
- Securing websites.
- Preventing network attacks.
- Protecting cloud infrastructure.

Real-world example:

Imagine a hospital.

Information Security:

- Protecting patient medical records.
- Locking confidential paper files.
- Giving access only to authorized staff.

Cybersecurity:

- Protecting hospital computers.
- Preventing ransomware attacks.
- Securing the hospital network.

Important relationship:

Cybersecurity is generally considered a specialized part of the broader information security field.

Remember:

- Information Security = Protecting information in every format.
- Cybersecurity = Protecting digital systems and environments against cyber threats.

Understanding these domains helps you identify different career opportunities in cybersecurity.

1\. Network Security

Protects computer networks from unauthorized access, attacks, and malicious activities.

Examples:

- Firewalls.
- IDS (Intrusion Detection System).
- IPS (Intrusion Prevention System).
- VPN.
- Network monitoring.

2\. Application Security

Protects software applications and websites from security vulnerabilities.

Examples:

- SQL Injection prevention.
- Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) prevention.
- Secure coding.
- Application testing.

3\. Cloud Security

Protects cloud environments, cloud infrastructure, applications, and stored data.

Examples:

- AWS security.
- Microsoft Azure security.
- Google Cloud security.
- IAM configuration.
- Cloud monitoring.

4\. Endpoint Security

Protects devices connected to a network.

Examples:

- Computers.
- Laptops.
- Mobile phones.
- Servers.

Tools and technologies:

- Antivirus.
- EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response).
- Device protection.

5\. Identity and Access Management (IAM)

Controls who can access systems, applications, and information.

Examples:

- User authentication.
- Authorization.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA).
- Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
- Least privilege.

6\. Security Operations (SOC)

Monitors, detects, investigates, and responds to cybersecurity threats.

Examples:

- Log monitoring.
- Alert investigation.
- Incident response.
- Threat detection.

7\. Penetration Testing and Ethical Hacking

Identifies security weaknesses by testing systems with proper authorization.

Examples:

- Vulnerability assessment.
- Web application penetration testing.
- Network penetration testing.
- Security testing.

8\. Cryptography

Protects information using mathematical techniques.

Examples:

- Encryption.
- Decryption.
- Hashing.
- Digital signatures.
- Secure communication.

9\. Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC)

Ensures organizations follow security policies, manage risks, and comply with relevant standards and regulations.

Examples:

- ISO 27001.
- Security policies.
- Risk assessment.
- Compliance audits.

## e. Modern Threat Landscape

Definition:

The modern threat landscape refers to the different types of cyber threats, attackers, attack techniques, and security risks that individuals and organizations face today.

In simple words, it means understanding what types of cyberattacks exist, how attackers perform them, and what damage they can cause.

### 1. Malware

Malware means malicious software designed to damage systems, steal information, or gain unauthorized access.

Types:

- Virus: Attaches itself to files and spreads when executed.
- Worm: Spreads automatically between systems.
- Trojan: Pretends to be legitimate software.
- Spyware: Secretly monitors user activities.
- Ransomware: Locks or encrypts files and demands payment.

Example: A user downloads an infected application, and malware steals personal information.

### 2. Phishing Attacks

Phishing is a technique where attackers send fake emails, messages, or websites to trick people into sharing sensitive information.

Example:

You receive an email that looks like it comes from your bank. It asks you to click a link and enter your username and password.

The website is fake, and the attacker steals your credentials.

### 3. Ransomware Attacks

Ransomware is malware that encrypts or locks files and demands money to restore access.

Example:

A company employee opens an infected attachment. The ransomware spreads through the company's systems and encrypts important business files.

### 4. DDoS Attacks

DDoS stands for Distributed Denial of Service.

Attackers use multiple devices or systems to send excessive traffic to a server, making the service unavailable to legitimate users.

Example:

An online shopping website receives huge amounts of malicious traffic and becomes unavailable.

### 5. Social Engineering

Social engineering is the technique of manipulating people into revealing confidential information or performing unsafe actions.

Example:

An attacker calls an employee pretending to be an IT administrator and asks for their login password.

### 6. Insider Threats

An insider threat occurs when someone with legitimate access to an organization's systems misuses that access, intentionally or accidentally.

Example:

An employee copies confidential company information and shares it with an unauthorized person.

### 7. Zero-Day Vulnerabilities

A zero-day vulnerability is a software security weakness that is exploited before the vendor has released an effective fix.

Example:

An attacker discovers an unknown vulnerability in a web application and exploits it before the organization can patch it.

### 8. AI-Powered Cyberattacks

Attackers can misuse Artificial Intelligence to improve their attacks.

Examples:

- Creating convincing phishing messages.
- Generating fake voices and deepfake videos.
- Automating reconnaissance.
- Creating more personalized social engineering attacks.

### 9. Supply Chain Attacks

A supply chain attack targets an organization's trusted suppliers, software providers, or service partners to compromise the organization.

Example:

An attacker compromises a software update from a trusted vendor. Organizations installing that update may become infected.

## Practical Scenario-Based Questions

These questions help you understand how cybersecurity concepts work in real-world situations.

Q1. An employee receives a fake bank email asking for login credentials. What type of attack is this?

Answer: Phishing attack.

Q2. A company wants to ensure that only authorized employees can access confidential files. Which security principle is involved?

Answer: Confidentiality.

Q3. A hacker encrypts company files and demands money to unlock them. What type of attack is this?

Answer: Ransomware attack.

Q4. An employee has permission to access company data but shares it with an unauthorized person. What is this called?

Answer: Insider threat.

Q5. A company uses firewalls and IDS to protect its internal network. Which cybersecurity domain is involved?

Answer: Network Security.

Q6. A hospital protects both physical patient records and digital medical records. Which security field covers both?

Answer: Information Security.

Q7. A website becomes unavailable because of excessive malicious traffic from multiple devices. What is the attack?

Answer: DDoS attack.

## Important Interview Questions and Answers

1\. What is cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, and digital information from cyberattacks and unauthorized access.

2\. What are the three main principles of cybersecurity?

Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA Triad).

3\. What is the difference between authentication and authorization?

- Authentication: Verifies who you are.
- Authorization: Determines what you are allowed to access.

4\. What is a cyberattack?

A cyberattack is an attempt to access, damage, disrupt, or steal information from digital systems without proper authorization.

5\. What is a vulnerability?

A vulnerability is a weakness in a system, application, or network that an attacker may exploit.

6\. What is a threat?

A threat is a potential danger that could exploit a vulnerability and cause harm.

7\. What is risk in cybersecurity?

Risk is the possibility of a threat exploiting a vulnerability and causing damage or loss.

8\. What is the difference between a hacker and an ethical hacker?

- Hacker: A person who uses technical skills to access or manipulate systems. The activity may be authorized or unauthorized.
- Ethical hacker: A security professional who tests systems with permission to identify vulnerabilities.
